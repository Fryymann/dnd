# Backstory

## Core premise

He is human, raised in a small village beside a forested lake by practical, ordinary people with remarkable depth. His mother was an herbalist. His father was a carpenter, voracious learner, and habitual tinkerer with a brilliant but largely unrecognized mind.

The family lived simply. Their son was never expected to remain there forever. His ability was evident early, and leaving was treated less as rejection of home than as the natural continuation of what his parents had given him.

At fifteen, already functioning mechanically as a level 1 Druid/Linewright, he traveled to a nearby port town and enlisted aboard a ship. He left home able to perform a small set of repeatable line configurations developed independently and with his parents' help. Work as a sailor and rigger then gave practical form to his gifts: tension, vectors, knots, counterweights, navigation, structural loads, weather, and the absolute requirement that complicated systems work under pressure.

He spent ten years at sea, returning to his family several times during most years rather than becoming estranged from home. During that decade he independently refined his Linewright practice. He has not met or been trained by another Linewright.

By the time the campaign begins, he is twenty-five, has reached level 3, and has developed the stable configurations represented by the Circle of Stars. His immediate location and reason for joining the adventure remain flexible so he can enter wherever Marc's campaign premise begins.

## Working timeline

- **Early childhood:** Sees cosmic lines before understanding that others cannot.
- **Childhood:** Parents gradually recognize the perception as consistent and real; the family begins observing and testing it together.
- **Before age 15:** Develops the repeatable techniques represented by his level-1 cantrips and spells.
- **Age 15:** Leaves the lakeside village, travels to the nearby port, and enlists aboard a ship as a level-1 Linewright/Druid.
- **Ages 15–25:** Works at sea, eventually specializing as a sailor and rigger; returns home several times most years; independently develops through levels 2 and 3.
- **Age 25:** Campaign begins at level 3 wherever Marc places the party.

## Family influences

### Mother — herbalist

- Taught him practical care, observation, patience, and respect for living systems.
- Healing was work done for neighbors, not a commodity reserved for temples or wealthy patrons.
- Her influence helps explain why his first instinct is to preserve life rather than exploit cosmic-line magic for status.

### Father — carpenter, learner, and tinkerer

- Possessed a brilliant mind without magical or supernatural assistance despite a simple life and lack of institutional recognition.
- Was fascinated by mathematics when it could be applied to real structures, tools, machines, measurements, and everyday problems.
- Taught through making, repairing, experimenting, and asking better questions.
- Carpentry gave his son an early language for geometry, hidden loads, bracing, and structural failure.
- Could not perceive cosmic lines. His importance lies in recognizing his son's intelligence, taking an impossible claim seriously, and helping develop methods to test and describe what the boy saw.

### Maternal grandfather — sailor and navigator

- Taught his daughter to navigate by the stars.
- Passed maritime knowledge into the family before his grandson ever saw a ship.
- His history helps connect the lakeside village, the family's love of the night sky, and the boy's eventual decision to go to sea.

## The family beneath the stars

Both parents loved the stars. His mother taught her husband and son the celestial navigation she had learned from her sailor father. The three of them often spent hours lying in the grass at night, looking upward together.

These nights were not religious observances. They combined affection, practical navigation, stories, mathematics, wonder, and quiet family companionship. They became the emotional foundation of his later spirituality.

He could see cosmic lines from early childhood. At first, his descriptions sounded like imagination or an unusual way of talking about starlight. It took years for his parents to recognize—and then comprehend—that he was consistently perceiving structures they could not see.

His father helped test the geometry and repeatability of what he described. His mother helped trace the lines' relationships to plants, health, animals, and other living things. By the time he left home at fifteen, the family had established that the lines connected the stars, cosmic energy, and life, even though the wider world had almost no framework for the phenomenon.

## Leaving home

The family always understood that he would eventually need a wider world. When he left at fifteen, the departure was expected and supported, though its emotional details remain open.

The nearby port connected his village upbringing to a larger life. A ship offered work without requiring credentials and surrounded him with practical mathematics that had immediate consequences. As a sailor and rigger, a correct calculation saved lives; an elegant explanation that failed under load was useless.

## Spirituality

He is not religious and does not worship a deity. He does not deny that gods exist; he rejects the assumption that they are the final explanation for reality.

Linewrights perceive cosmic energy connecting all life. To him, this is direct experience rather than doctrine. The gods, the Weave, mortal life, and the planes may all be structures within something larger that has not yet been understood.

His spirituality is private, grounded, and non-institutional:

- All living things participate in a larger connected reality.
- Seeing a connection creates some responsibility toward it.
- Mathematics can describe a relationship without exhausting its meaning.
- Knowledge should produce humility because every solved structure reveals a larger one.
- Healing matters because damage to one life sends strain through other connected lives.

Mathematics tells him how to manipulate cosmic lines. Spirituality tells him why preserving their living connections matters.

## Linewright identity

"Linewright" is the preferred name for the rare practitioners able to perceive and manipulate cosmic lines.

His shipboard vocabulary naturally overlaps with the discipline:

- set or slacken a line;
- brace a pattern;
- redistribute strain;
- establish an anchor;
- close a circuit;
- clear a fouled crossing;
- prevent a cascading failure.

He may describe profound magical operations in the same practical language used to rig a mast or stabilize a load.
