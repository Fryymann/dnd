## 1. Seed

Capture the raw idea before improving it.

- Initial prompt or inspiration: Design the first of several possible characters for a campaign that may start in a few weeks: an unusual healer who splits healing with ally enhancement.
- Desired fantasy: Keep the group functional and make allies better at what they do, without repeating a familiar cleric or paladin experience.
- Desired emotional tone: Open; still to be discovered through the character concept.
- Memorable visual, behavior, or scene: Not decided yet.
- Known constraints: No paladin. Cleric is acceptable only if unconventional. D&D 5.5e is the working ruleset; campaign and source details are not yet known.

## 2. Exploration

Use only the questions that matter for this concept. Add, remove, or reorder them freely.

### Play experience

- What should this character regularly get to do that feels satisfying?
- What should their turns or scenes feel like: decisive, clever, dangerous, protective, strange, social, or something else?
- How much mechanical complexity and resource tracking sounds enjoyable?

### Story and personality

- What do they want, and what makes that difficult?
- What contradiction makes them more than their archetype?
- What belief, bond, fear, or flaw is likely to create useful choices at the table?
- Why would they stay with an adventuring party when things become costly?

### World connection

- What parts of the campaign premise or setting should they naturally engage with?
- Which relationships, factions, duties, debts, or mysteries already pull on them?
- What should remain open for the GM and play to define?

### Mechanical expression

- Which parts of the fantasy must be represented mechanically rather than only through flavor?
- Is there a preferred role, signature action, class, species, background, feat, item, or spell?
- What mechanics would undermine the desired fantasy even if they are powerful?

## 4. Chosen concept

- Character premise: A male sailor and rigger with a genius-level intuitive command of mathematics. Raised beside a forested lake by an herbalist mother and a brilliant carpenter/tinkerer father, he became a Linewright: one of the rare people able to perceive cosmic energy connecting all life. He deliberately changes that geometry to heal, defend, enhance, or harm.
- Central tension: He possesses a form of arcane mastery that established institutions barely recognize, but he lacks—or rejects—the credentials and social position they use to define expertise. His private spirituality also tells him that mastery does not equal complete understanding: cosmic connection extends beyond the gods into a reality mortals have barely begun to perceive.
- Party contribution: Primary healer and caregiver; defensive support and concentration buffs; durable midfield presence; ranged radiant damage when the group is stable.
- Signature behavior or imagery: Chalk calculations on work surfaces, improvised measuring tools, geometric diagrams that experts struggle to follow, and luminous cosmic lines that only he can naturally perceive. He may solve complex transformations mentally while performing ordinary manual work.
- Mechanical promise: Circle of Stars modes allow him to intensify healing, protect concentration, or contribute ranged damage without relying on beast-form combat.
- Narrative promise: His existence challenges Forgotten Realms assumptions about the Weave, divinity, and who is entitled to be called a mage. Family inheritance, shipboard life, rare Linewrights, institutional disbelief, and the larger reality beyond the gods provide hooks without making him a chosen priest or detached calculating prodigy.

### Cosmic-line magic model

- **Source:** Cosmic energy that exists beyond and independently of the Weave.
- **Access:** A rare innate perceptual ability allows certain people to see cosmic lines directly.
- **Practice:** Practitioners use mathematics to understand tension, intersection, flow, resonance, and transformation within those lines.
- **Healing:** He restores a living system's damaged geometry, reconnects interrupted flows, or supplies cosmic energy along a stable pattern. Exact terminology remains open.
- **Starry Form:** Chalice, Dragon, and Archer are three proven line configurations rather than literal constellations or astrological signs.
- **Druid classification:** Circle of Stars is the game-mechanical chassis; in-world scholars may classify him incorrectly because no established class describes the discipline well.
- **Rules boundary:** Unless the GM explicitly approves otherwise, cosmic-line effects remain mechanically subject to normal spell rules, Counterspell, antimagic, and similar interactions. The different source is narrative, not a free mechanical immunity.
- **Spiritual meaning:** Cosmic lines reveal that all life is connected within a reality larger than gods or mortal cosmology. Mathematics describes the structure; it does not reduce living beings to numbers.
- **Practitioner name:** Linewright.

## 6. Conversation log

Append a section for each substantial design conversation.

### 2026-07-25 — Druid healer chassis

- **Starting question:** What should distinguish this healer from familiar cleric and paladin characters?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** Use Druid; make healing and care primary, with defense and buffing for a damage-heavy party. Avoid Wild Shape as the main feature. Use feats and species traits creatively to create an off-brand class, then add tankiness or decent damage.
- **What it implies:** Favor Druid circles that convert Wild Shape into a non-beast mode or companion. Evaluate the build as a healer first and its secondary combat package second.
- **Options considered:** Circle of Stars, Circle of Wildfire, and a durable 2024 Warden chassis with subclass still open.
- **Decision or current lean:** Druid is selected; Stars currently offers the cleanest stance-switching package, while Wildfire offers the strongest rescue-specialist identity.
- **Still open:** Tankiness versus damage, campaign source policy, starting level, and the preferred emotional/aesthetic interpretation of care.

### 2026-07-25 — Earlier astronomer direction (superseded)

- The initial version treated him as an astronomer/scientist using a mixture of astronomy, arcana, and astrology while investigating an unknown source of power.
- This was superseded by the cosmic-line mathematician direction below. It remains recorded only to preserve the design history.

### 2026-07-25 — Cosmic-line mathematician retcon

- **Starting question:** What if he understands the power and it operates through cosmic energy rather than the Weave?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** Remove astrology and the unknown-source premise. He is a rare cosmic-line practitioner who understands the mathematics of an independent form of magic. His personality and social position are inspired by Will Hunting: blue collar, genius-minded, and not a stereotypical math nerd.
- **What it implies:** His conflict comes from class, institutions, recognition, and responsibility rather than ignorance about his own abilities. His magic should look like practical manipulation of visible geometry, not prayer, natural mysticism, or horoscope symbolism.
- **Options considered:** Cosmic lines as a parallel magical substrate, an energy network beyond the Weave, or observable higher-dimensional geometry.
- **Decision or current lean:** Cosmic energy is the source; rare perception is the gate; mathematics is the method. Standard D&D spell interactions remain intact unless the GM approves mechanical exceptions.
- **Still open:** His trade, educational history, temperament, reason for becoming a caregiver, species, and whether any other practitioner taught him formal techniques.

### 2026-07-25 — Family, spirituality, and the sea

- **Starting question:** What keeps his mathematical genius connected to people, and what life produced his blue-collar identity?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** He is nonreligious but deeply spiritual in private. Linewrights see cosmic energy connecting all life and recognize that reality extends beyond the gods. His herbalist mother and brilliant carpenter/tinkerer father raised him simply near a forested lake. Everyone knew he would leave; at fifteen he went to a nearby port and enlisted aboard a ship.
- **What it implies:** His mother grounds healing in community care, his father grounds genius in craft and curiosity, and sailing turns cosmic-line theory into practical knowledge of tension, navigation, and responsibility under pressure.
- **Options considered:** Dockworker, rigger, and sailor all fit; these can be successive roles rather than exclusive choices.
- **Decision or current lean:** He becomes a sailor whose specialty is rigging, with dock work as part of his early or intermittent career.
- **Still open:** Inheritance of the gift, first deliberate lineworking, shipboard mentor and relationships, career length, departure from the sea, and route into adventuring.

### 2026-07-25 — Childhood sight and level progression

- **Starting question:** Did his father share the gift, how did the family understand it, and what could he do when he left home?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** His father was brilliant without supernatural aid and loved real-world mathematics. Both parents loved the stars. His maternal grandfather was a sailor who taught his mother celestial navigation, which she taught to her husband and son. The family spent hours stargazing together. The boy always saw cosmic lines; his parents took years to realize he was describing a real perception unavailable to them. By fifteen, they understood that the lines connected stars and living things. He left home at level 1 with two cantrips and four prepared spells, then reaches level 3 before the campaign begins.
- **What it implies:** The parents are collaborators in his early Linewright practice without being magical mentors. His first repertoire should reflect repair, celestial projection, herbal care, healing at touch and range, and revealing hidden lines.
- **Options considered:** Mending versus Thorn Whip for the second cantrip; Goodberry versus a defensive spell among the first four prepared spells.
- **Decision or current lean:** Begin with Mending and Starry Wisp; prepare Cure Wounds, Healing Word, Goodberry, and Faerie Fire. Speak with Animals is always prepared through the 2024 Druidic feature.
- **Still open:** Confirm the working spell choices, identify the first effect he ever produced, and determine what he learned during his years at sea.

### 2026-07-25 — A decade at sea

- **Starting question:** How old is he at campaign start, how long has he sailed, and did another Linewright train him?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** He is twenty-five and has spent ten years at sea since leaving home at fifteen, returning home a few times most years. He reached level 3 entirely through his own development and has not encountered another Linewright. His starting location should remain open for Marc's campaign.
- **What it implies:** He is an experienced working sailor rather than a sheltered prodigy. His family remains emotionally current. His Starry Form is the mature result of a decade spent testing cosmic geometry against navigation, rigging, weather, injury, and shipboard life.
- **Options considered:** Short intense maritime career versus a full decade; independent development versus another practitioner.
- **Decision or current lean:** Ten-year independent development with periodic returns home.
- **Still open:** Ships and crews, notable voyage events, adult personality, species, feats, and Marc's campaign hook.

### 2026-07-25 — Bedside manner

- **Starting question:** Is he reassuring and openly compassionate or a blunt sailor whose actions are gentler than his words?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** Blunt sailor, definitely.
- **What it implies:** He gives direct instructions, states danger honestly, and uses dry humor during treatment. Care appears through competence, preparation, memory, and follow-up rather than sentimental language.
- **Options considered:** Warm physician versus blunt shipboard medic.
- **Decision or current lean:** Rough with bad decisions, careful with injured people.
- **Still open:** How abrasive he becomes around authority, whether he hides concern behind humor, and which personal flaw creates the best roleplaying tension.

### 2026-07-25 — Human and custom background (superseded)

- **Starting question:** Which species and legal custom-background package support the feat-driven Linewright build?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** Human is preferred because of the extra feat. Custom backgrounds are allowed but must use an existing background feature and one of three proficiency structures: 2 skills + 2 tools, 2 skills + 2 languages, or 2 skills + 1 tool + 1 language.
- **What it implies:** Use Human's Versatile Origin feat and import Healer from an existing background package rather than creating a new feat. The two-tool structure best preserves navigation, rigging, carpentry, and practical competence.
- **Options considered:** Tools versus languages; Healer versus Magic Initiate as the background feat; Tough versus additional arcane utility.
- **Decision at the time:** Custom Ship's Linewright with Healer from the background and Magic Initiate from Human.
- **Superseded by:** The Sailor decision below.

### 2026-07-25 — Sailor background and Human Healer

- **Starting question:** Should the mechanical background be customized for feats or reflect his actual profession directly?
- **Ian's answer or direction:** Use the Sailor background for its thematic fit. Take Healer through Human and push Magic Initiate to a later level.
- **What it implies:** His level-1 feat package contains only Healer. His background supplies Athletics, Perception, Navigator's Tools, water-vehicle proficiency, and the Sailor feature/package used by Marc. Arcane expansion remains earned future growth rather than part of his childhood repertoire.
- **Options considered:** Custom Ship's Linewright versus standard/legacy Sailor.
- **Decision or current lean:** Sailor with Ship's Passage under the legacy feature model; Human Versatile grants Healer. Magic Initiate becomes a level-4-or-later candidate rather than a commitment.
- **Still open:** Marc's exact Sailor equipment implementation and whether level 4 favors Magic Initiate, War Caster, or a Wisdom increase.
