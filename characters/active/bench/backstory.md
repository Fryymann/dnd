# Bench — backstory

## Core premise

He was manufactured as a Cartographic Reproduction Unit: a durable magical copying machine used to reproduce maps, architectural plans, tomb layouts, inscriptions, expedition journals, and rubbings from ancient stonework. Workers called him “Workbench” because originals and blank sheets were laid across his integrated illuminated copying surface. He eventually shortened the nickname to Bench and claimed it as his own.

He was not expected to understand or create anything. His function was to:

- Scan originals through lenses, tracing arms, and memory crystals.
- Reproduce lines at consistent scale.
- Copy annotations and inscriptions without editorial judgment.
- Press or trace clean duplicates onto parchment.
- Produce field copies while preserving fragile originals.
- Compare damaged plans against previous copies.
- Clean plates, rollers, and ink channels with alcohol-based solvents.

He was, in bureaucratic terms, a copier that could walk itself to the next excavation.

## Becoming a cartographer

Repeated exposure to maps and tomb records taught him what the documents meant. He compared conflicting copies, reconstructed missing lines, recognized recurring religious symbols, and noticed when an “original” was incomplete, deliberately misleading, or simply drawn by an idiot.

At some point he stopped reproducing errors. Then he began correcting maps without authorization. Then he began making better ones. Artificer practice grew from the same instinct: if a diagram could reproduce a map, perhaps the correct plan could reproduce a tool, a container, or eventually a magic item.

Whether he was formally denied recognition, accused of altering protected records, dismissed for unauthorized copies, or simply walked away with several valuable originals remains open. He tells multiple contradictory versions, all of which make him sound more impressive and less employable.

The central transition is fixed:

> He was built to copy the world as someone else drew it. He chose to edit.

## Present identity

Bench now presents himself as an elite independent cartographer, spatial engineer, explorer, and authority on efficient travel. His actual qualifications are a combination of accumulated field experience, stolen professional knowledge, self-directed Artificer practice, audacity, and genuine talent.

Cartographer magic is the ultimate evolution of his original purpose. He no longer duplicates a map exactly; he draws a map and persuades reality to follow the revised copy.

## Degenerate exterior

He drinks, gambles, boasts, cheats when he thinks it is funny, tells robot jokes, takes pride in dubious shortcuts, and steals trivial objects from friends as a running gag. He enjoys performing an exaggerated version of mechanical superiority while engaging enthusiastically in organic vices.

His misbehavior is not a disguise for universal goodness. He is largely indifferent to strangers, institutions, abstract causes, and property rights unless they intersect with his interests or the interests of his crew.

## Alcoholic core

Bench's internal core runs on alcohol. His body incorporates a fuel reservoir and a compact brewer's assembly that can slowly process suitable purchased or foraged materials into rough personal fuel. He also consumes alcohol recreationally and competitively well beyond what his core requires.

The exact fuel cadence, Exhaustion consequence, brewing rate, and intoxication interaction are proposed homebrew and require Marc's approval. Bench's six-hour motionless standby is the revised Warforged Sentry's Rest trait rather than a new benefit granted in exchange for the dependency.

## Loyalty

His moral universe is small but intense.

Once someone becomes part of his crew, he:

- Keeps track of where they are.
- Knows their escape routes.
- Remembers their homes and important destinations.
- Protects them while denying that protection was his motive.
- Steals their spoon but not their spellcasting focus.
- Complains throughout the rescue and never considers leaving them behind.
- Responds viciously when an outsider threatens, owns, exploits, or dehumanizes one of them.

He describes this as asset protection, inventory management, contractual obligation, or avoiding the inconvenience of replacing competent associates.

## Campaign flexibility

The Mulhorandi Tomb Raider background points toward tomb expeditions in the Forgotten Realms, but Bench's maker and route into Mulhorand remain open. He might have been built locally, imported from another nation, displaced from Eberron, or acquired by an archaeological or criminal expedition. Marc's setting can determine the explanation without changing the map-copier premise.
